1. National Income

National income measures the value of final goods and services generated by an economy during a period. GDP measures production within domestic territory; GNP adjusts GDP for net factor income from abroad.

Methods

  • Value Added Method
  • Income Method
  • Expenditure Method
Real GDP uses constant prices; Nominal GDP uses current prices.

2. Money

Money is anything generally accepted as a means of payment. It solves the problems of barter by providing a common medium of exchange.

  • Medium of exchange
  • Measure of value
  • Store of value
  • Standard of deferred payments

3. Banking & Central Bank

Commercial banks accept deposits and provide loans. The central bank regulates the banking system and uses monetary policy to influence credit and liquidity.

Important tools include changes in policy rates, reserve requirements and open market operations, as applicable.

4. Determination of Income and Employment

Aggregate demand represents planned expenditure in an economy. Equilibrium output occurs when planned aggregate demand equals output.

Multiplier = 1 ÷ (1 − MPC), in the simple model.

MPC is marginal propensity to consume. A rise in autonomous expenditure can create a multiplied change in equilibrium income.

5. Government Budget

A government budget is an annual statement of estimated receipts and expenditure.

  • Revenue receipts and capital receipts
  • Revenue expenditure and capital expenditure
  • Revenue deficit, fiscal deficit and primary deficit
Fiscal Deficit = Total Expenditure − Total Receipts excluding borrowings

6. Balance of Payments

Balance of Payments records economic transactions between residents of an economy and the rest of the world.

  • Current account: goods, services, income and transfers.
  • Capital/financial account: transactions affecting external assets and liabilities, as classified in the syllabus.

Exchange rate is the price of one currency in terms of another.

7. Indian Economy on the Eve of Independence

The colonial economy had low industrial development, limited infrastructure for broad-based development and heavy dependence on agriculture. Per-capita income and living conditions were poor.

8. Indian Economy 1950–1990

India adopted planned development with emphasis on public sector, industrialisation, self-reliance and development of agriculture. The Green Revolution increased food-grain production in selected regions.

9. Economic Reforms Since 1991

Reforms included liberalisation, privatisation and globalisation. The aim was to reduce unnecessary controls, increase competition and integrate the economy more with the world economy.

10. Human Capital Formation

Human capital is the stock of knowledge, skills and health embodied in people. Education and healthcare improve productivity and can support economic development.

11. Rural Development

Rural development includes measures for better credit, marketing, infrastructure, diversification of livelihoods and improved productivity.

12. Employment

Employment concepts include worker, labour force, unemployment and different employment structures. Policies aim to create productive and sustainable work opportunities.

13. Poverty

Poverty is a condition in which people lack sufficient resources to meet basic needs. Its causes can include low income, unemployment, unequal opportunities and inadequate access to education and health.

14. Environment & Sustainable Development

Sustainable development seeks to meet present needs without reducing the ability of future generations to meet their needs. It requires efficient resource use, pollution control and balanced economic growth.
