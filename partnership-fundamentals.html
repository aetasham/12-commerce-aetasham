1. Meaning of Partnership

Partnership is a relationship between persons who agree to carry on a business and share its profits according to an agreed arrangement. The important accounting idea is that the partners jointly own the business and each partner may act for the firm in the ordinary course of business.

Core elements

  • Agreement between persons.
  • Business activity.
  • Agreement to share profits.
  • Mutual agency.
  • Partners' rights and duties are governed by the agreement and applicable partnership law.

2. Partnership Deed

A partnership deed is the written agreement containing the terms accepted by the partners.

Usually includes

  • Name and nature of business.
  • Names of partners.
  • Capital contribution.
  • Profit and loss sharing ratio.
  • Drawings and their limits.
  • Interest on capital and drawings.
  • Partner salary, commission or bonus.
  • Admission, retirement and death arrangements.
  • Rules for settlement and dispute handling.
Exam rule: Read the deed first. If a term is missing, apply the applicable default rule instead of assuming a special treatment.

3. If the Partnership Deed is Silent

For school-level questions, common default treatments are:

  • Profit and loss are shared equally when no ratio is specified.
  • A partner is not automatically entitled to salary or commission merely for working for the firm.
  • Interest on capital is not automatically allowed merely because capital was introduced.
  • Interest on drawings is not automatically charged merely because drawings were made.
  • Interest on a partner's loan is treated separately from interest on capital according to the applicable rules.

4. Capital Accounts

Fixed Capital Method

Capital accounts normally remain at the agreed amount. Routine adjustments such as interest on capital, salary, commission, drawings and share of profit/loss are generally recorded through Current Accounts.

Fluctuating Capital Method

A single capital account records capital plus routine adjustments, so the balance changes from period to period.

Quick difference: Fixed = capital stays fixed, current account handles routine adjustments. Fluctuating = one capital account keeps changing.

5. Profit and Loss Appropriation Account

This account explains how the firm's profit is appropriated among partners after the net profit has been determined.

Typical appropriations include interest on capital, partner salary/commission and transfer of the remaining profit to partners in the agreed ratio.

Interest = Amount × Rate × Time / 100

6. Interest on Capital

Interest on capital is allowed when the partnership terms or applicable default rule provides for it.

Interest on Capital = Capital × Rate × Time / 100

If capital changes during the year, calculate interest separately for each relevant period.

Example: Capital ₹1,00,000 at 10% for a full year gives ₹10,000 interest.

7. Interest on Drawings

Interest on drawings is charged when the agreement provides for it. The calculation depends on amount, rate and period of use.

Interest on Drawings = Drawings × Rate × Time / 100

For repeated equal drawings, an average-period shortcut may be used when the question permits it. Always identify the timing of drawings first.

8. Partner Salary, Commission and Bonus

When provided by the partnership terms, salary, commission or bonus payable to a partner is an appropriation of profit. Calculate it exactly according to the wording of the question.

If commission is calculated after charging commission, use the stated base carefully rather than simply multiplying the original profit by the rate.

9. Guarantee of Profit

A partner may be guaranteed a minimum amount of profit. If the actual share is lower, the deficiency is borne by the partner(s) responsible for the guarantee according to the agreed terms.

Deficiency = Guaranteed Amount − Actual Profit Share
Example: Guarantee ₹20,000 and actual share ₹16,000 → deficiency ₹4,000.

10. Past Adjustments

Past adjustments correct earlier omissions or incorrect recording of items such as interest on capital, interest on drawings, salary or commission.

  1. Calculate what each partner should have received or paid.
  2. Compare with what was actually recorded.
  3. Find the net difference.
  4. Pass the adjustment through partners' capital/current accounts.

11. Important Ratio Concepts

Sacrificing Ratio = Old Share − New Share
Gaining Ratio = New Share − Old Share

A partner sacrifices when their share falls and gains when their share rises.

12. Numerical Practice

Q1. A and B share profits 3:2. Firm profit is ₹50,000. Find each partner's share.
Answer: A ₹30,000; B ₹20,000.
Q2. Capital is ₹2,00,000 at 8% p.a. for one year. Find interest.
Answer: ₹16,000.
Q3. A partner is guaranteed ₹25,000 but receives ₹21,000. Find deficiency.
Answer: ₹4,000.
Q4. A and B share 1:1 and change to 3:2. Find the change in each share.
Answer: A gains 1/10; B sacrifices 1/10.

13. Important Exam Questions

  1. Define partnership and explain its essential features.
  2. What is a partnership deed? State its important contents.
  3. Explain the accounting treatment when the deed is silent.
  4. Distinguish between fixed and fluctuating capital accounts.
  5. Calculate interest on capital and interest on drawings.
  6. Explain guarantee of profit with a numerical example.
  7. Prepare a past-adjustment calculation for omitted items.
  8. Calculate sacrificing or gaining ratio.

14. MCQ Practice

1. Which concept is central to partnership?
A) Mutual agency   B) Only salary   C) Only capital   D) Only drawings
Answer: A
2. Under fixed capital method, routine adjustments are generally recorded in:
A) Trading A/c   B) Current A/c   C) Cash Flow A/c   D) Sales A/c
Answer: B
3. If old share is 1/2 and new share is 1/3, sacrifice is:
A) 1/6   B) 1/3   C) 1/2   D) 2/3
Answer: A
4. Interest on capital is calculated using:
A) Capital × Rate × Time / 100   B) Capital + Rate + Time   C) Profit ÷ Capital   D) Drawings × Profit
Answer: A
5. Past adjustment mainly corrects:
A) Earlier omissions/errors   B) New sales   C) Bank balance only   D) Closing stock only
Answer: A

15. Quick Revision Sheet

  • First: Read the partnership deed.
  • Then: identify ratio, capital, drawings and appropriations.
  • Interest: Amount × Rate × Time / 100.
  • Guarantee: compare guaranteed amount with actual share.
  • Past adjustment: should be minus actually recorded.
  • Ratio change: old share versus new share gives sacrifice/gain.
