1. Partnership Fundamentals

Meaning: Partnership is an agreement between persons who carry on a business and agree to share its profits. Each partner acts for the firm and the other partners.

Partnership Deed

The deed records capital, drawings, profit sharing, salary, commission and interest terms.

Important Accounting

  • Fixed capital: adjustments normally go through Current Account.
  • Fluctuating capital: capital changes with profit, drawings and adjustments.
  • P&L Appropriation Account: distributes profit among partners.
  • Past adjustment: corrects omissions or errors from earlier periods.
Partner's Share of Profit = Total Profit × Partner's Profit-Sharing Fraction
Example: A and B share profit 3:2. Profit ₹50,000. A = ₹30,000; B = ₹20,000.

2. Goodwill

Goodwill is the value of a firm's reputation and customer relationships that can support higher future profits.

Average Profit Method

Goodwill = Average Profit × Years' Purchase
Average Profit = Total Adjusted Profits ÷ Number of Years

Super Profit Method

Normal Profit = Capital Employed × Normal Rate ÷ 100
Super Profit = Average Profit − Normal Profit
Goodwill = Super Profit × Years' Purchase

Capitalisation Method

Capitalised Value = Average Profit × 100 ÷ Normal Rate
Goodwill = Capitalised Value − Actual Capital Employed
Example: Average profit ₹60,000, normal rate 10%, capital employed ₹5,00,000. Normal profit = ₹50,000; super profit = ₹10,000.

3. Change in Profit-Sharing Ratio

When existing partners change their ratio, one may sacrifice while another gains.

Sacrificing Ratio = Old Share − New Share
Gaining Ratio = New Share − Old Share

Goodwill, revaluation and reserves are adjusted according to the reconstitution terms.

4. Admission of a Partner

A new partner enters an existing firm and the partnership is reconstituted.

  1. Calculate new ratio.
  2. Calculate sacrificing ratio.
  3. Adjust goodwill.
  4. Revalue assets and liabilities.
  5. Adjust reserves and capitals.
  6. Prepare revised Balance Sheet.

5. Retirement of a Partner

An existing partner leaves while the remaining partners continue.

Gaining Ratio = New Share − Old Share

Adjust goodwill, revaluation, reserves and the amount due to the retiring partner.

6. Death of a Partner

The deceased partner's estate is entitled to the amount due under the partnership terms.

  • Capital balance
  • Share of goodwill
  • Revaluation profit/loss
  • Reserves
  • Profit up to date of death

7. Dissolution of Partnership Firm

Dissolution closes the business and settles assets and liabilities.

  • Assets are transferred to Realisation Account as applicable.
  • External liabilities are settled.
  • Realisation profit/loss goes to partners in profit-sharing ratio.
  • Remaining cash is distributed according to final balances.

8. Share Capital

Share capital is money raised by a company through shares.

  • Application
  • Allotment
  • Calls
  • Oversubscription and pro-rata allotment
  • Calls in arrears
  • Forfeiture and reissue

9. Debentures

Debentures are long-term debt instruments. Holders are creditors of the company.

  • Issue at par/premium/discount according to terms.
  • Interest is a finance cost.
  • Redemption means repayment of debenture liability.

10. Financial Statements

Statement of Profit and Loss

Shows income, expenses and profit/loss for a period.

Balance Sheet

Shows financial position at a particular date.

Notes to Accounts

Provide supporting disclosures for financial statement figures.

11. Analysis of Financial Statements

  • Comparative statements
  • Common-size statements
  • Trend analysis
  • Interpretation of financial performance

12. Accounting Ratios

Current Ratio = Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities
Quick Ratio = Quick Assets ÷ Current Liabilities
Debt-Equity Ratio = Long-term Debt ÷ Shareholders' Funds
Gross Profit Ratio = Gross Profit ÷ Revenue from Operations × 100
Net Profit Ratio = Net Profit ÷ Revenue from Operations × 100

13. Cash Flow Statement

Explains changes in cash and cash equivalents.

  • Operating: main revenue-generating activities.
  • Investing: long-term assets and investments.
  • Financing: share capital and borrowings.

Under the indirect method, accounting profit is adjusted for non-cash items and working-capital changes.
