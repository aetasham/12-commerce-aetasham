1. Partnership Fundamentals

Partnership is an agreement between persons to carry on a business and share profits. Important topics include partnership deed, fixed and fluctuating capital, Current Account, Profit and Loss Appropriation Account, interest on capital, interest on drawings, partner salary/commission and past adjustments.

Partnership Deed

It records capital, profit-sharing ratio, drawings, interest, salary, commission and other agreed terms.

Partner Share = Profit × Partner's Profit-Sharing Fraction

Example: A:B = 3:2 and profit = ₹50,000. A gets ₹30,000 and B gets ₹20,000.

2. Goodwill

Goodwill represents the reputation and other advantages of a business that help it earn higher profits.

Goodwill = Average Profit × Years' Purchase
Super Profit = Average Profit − Normal Profit
Normal Profit = Capital Employed × Normal Rate ÷ 100
Goodwill = Super Profit × Years' Purchase
Capitalised Value = Average Profit × 100 ÷ Normal Rate

Goodwill is adjusted during admission, retirement, death or change in ratio according to the applicable ratio.

3. Change in Profit-Sharing Ratio

Existing partners may change their ratio without admitting a new partner.

Sacrificing Ratio = Old Share − New Share
Gaining Ratio = New Share − Old Share

Adjust goodwill, reserves, accumulated profits/losses and revaluation as required.

4. Admission of a Partner

A new partner is admitted with the consent of existing partners.

  1. Calculate new ratio.
  2. Calculate sacrificing ratio.
  3. Adjust goodwill.
  4. Revalue assets and liabilities.
  5. Adjust reserves and accumulated profits/losses.
  6. Adjust capitals.
  7. Prepare revised Balance Sheet.

5. Retirement of a Partner

A partner retires while the remaining partners continue the business.

Gaining Ratio = New Share − Old Share

Calculate goodwill, revaluation, reserves, capital and the amount payable to the retiring partner.

6. Death of a Partner

The deceased partner's estate receives the amount due under the partnership agreement.

  • Capital balance
  • Goodwill share
  • Revaluation profit/loss
  • Reserves
  • Profit up to date of death
  • Less drawings and other applicable deductions

7. Dissolution of Partnership Firm

Dissolution closes the business and settles assets and liabilities.

  • Assets are transferred to Realisation Account as applicable.
  • External liabilities are settled.
  • Realisation profit/loss is transferred to partners.
  • Partner loans are settled.
  • Capital accounts are settled and remaining cash is distributed.

8. Share Capital

Share capital is raised by a company through issue of shares.

  • Authorised, issued, subscribed, called-up and paid-up capital
  • Application and allotment
  • Calls
  • Oversubscription and pro-rata allotment
  • Calls in arrears/advance
  • Forfeiture and reissue

9. Debentures

Debentures are long-term debt instruments and their holders are creditors.

  • Issue at par
  • Issue at premium
  • Issue at discount where applicable
  • Issue for consideration other than cash
  • Interest on debentures
  • Redemption

10. Financial Statements of a Company

Balance Sheet

Shows the financial position of a company on a particular date.

Statement of Profit and Loss

Shows income, expenses and profit/loss for an accounting period.

Notes to Accounts

Give supporting details and disclosures for financial statement items.

11. Analysis of Financial Statements

Analysis helps evaluate liquidity, solvency, efficiency and profitability.

  • Comparative Statements
  • Common-size Statements
  • Ratio Analysis
  • Cash Flow Analysis

12. Accounting Ratios

Current Ratio = Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities
Quick Ratio = Quick Assets ÷ Current Liabilities
Debt-Equity Ratio = Long-term Debt ÷ Shareholders' Funds
Gross Profit Ratio = Gross Profit ÷ Revenue from Operations × 100
Net Profit Ratio = Net Profit ÷ Revenue from Operations × 100
Inventory Turnover Ratio = Cost of Revenue from Operations ÷ Average Inventory
Working Capital Turnover Ratio = Revenue from Operations ÷ Working Capital

13. Cash Flow Statement

Cash Flow Statement explains changes in cash and cash equivalents.

Operating Activities

Main revenue-generating business activities.

Investing Activities

Purchase and sale of long-term assets and investments.

Financing Activities

Changes in share capital and borrowings and other financing flows.

Under the indirect method, accounting profit is adjusted for non-cash items, non-operating items and working-capital changes.
