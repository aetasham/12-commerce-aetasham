1. Partnership Fundamentals

Meaning: Partnership is an agreement between persons who carry on a business and agree to share its profits. Each partner acts for the firm and for the other partners.

Partnership Deed

The deed is the written agreement containing rules about capital, drawings, profit sharing, salary, commission and interest. If a point is not stated, the applicable partnership law/rules are followed.

Important Accounting

  • Fixed capital: Capital normally remains unchanged; adjustments such as salary, interest and drawings go through Current Account.
  • Fluctuating capital: Capital itself changes with profit, drawings, interest and other adjustments.
  • P&L Appropriation A/c: Shows distribution of profit among partners after determining net profit.
  • Past adjustment: Used to correct omitted/wrongly recorded items from earlier periods.
Example: A and B share profit 3:2. Profit is ₹50,000. A's share = ₹30,000 and B's share = ₹20,000.

2. Goodwill

Goodwill is the value of a firm's reputation that helps it earn higher profits than a similar business. It is affected by location, quality, customer loyalty, management and expected future profits.

Average Profit Method

Goodwill = Average Profit × Years' Purchase

Average Profit = Total adjusted profits ÷ Number of years.

Super Profit Method

Normal Profit = Capital Employed × Normal Rate ÷ 100
Super Profit = Average Profit − Normal Profit
Goodwill = Super Profit × Years' Purchase

Capitalisation Method

Capitalised Value = Average Profit × 100 ÷ Normal Rate
Goodwill = Capitalised Value − Actual Capital Employed
Example: Average profit ₹60,000, normal rate 10%, capital employed ₹5,00,000. Normal profit = ₹50,000; super profit = ₹10,000.

3. Change in Profit-Sharing Ratio

Existing partners may change their ratio. The partner whose share falls sacrifices; the partner whose share rises gains.

Sacrificing Ratio = Old Share − New Share
Gaining Ratio = New Share − Old Share
  • Goodwill is adjusted between sacrificing and gaining partners as required.
  • Revaluation profit/loss is transferred to old partners in the old ratio when the change is treated as a reconstitution.
  • Reserves and accumulated profits/losses are generally adjusted in the old ratio.
Example: A changes from 1/2 to 1/3. A sacrifices 1/6 of the profit share.

4. Admission of a Partner

When a new partner enters, the firm is reconstituted. The new partner usually brings capital and goodwill.

Steps

  1. Calculate new ratio and sacrificing ratio.
  2. Adjust goodwill according to the question.
  3. Revalue assets and liabilities.
  4. Distribute reserves/accumulated profits or losses.
  5. Adjust capitals and prepare the revised Balance Sheet.
Exam tip: Always write the old ratio, new ratio and sacrificing ratio before passing goodwill entries.

5. Retirement of a Partner

Retirement means an existing partner leaves while the remaining partners continue the business.

  • Calculate the new ratio of continuing partners.
  • Calculate gaining ratio.
  • Adjust goodwill, revaluation and reserves.
  • Calculate the retiring partner's final amount due.
  • Settlement may be through cash/bank or a loan account, depending on the terms.
Gaining Ratio = New Share − Old Share

6. Death of a Partner

Death is treated as a change in the partnership. The deceased partner's estate is entitled to the amount due.

Amount Due May Include

  • Capital balance.
  • Share of goodwill.
  • Share of revaluation profit/loss.
  • Share of reserves and accumulated profits/losses.
  • Share of profit up to the date of death.
  • Interest or other amounts as specified.

The amount due is transferred to the executor's account and settled according to the agreement.

7. Dissolution of Partnership Firm

Dissolution means the business is closed and assets/liabilities are settled.

Realisation Account

  • Assets are transferred to Realisation Account except cash/bank and items specifically excluded.
  • External liabilities are transferred and settled.
  • Profit or loss on realisation is transferred to partners' capital accounts in their profit-sharing ratio.
  • Cash/Bank records actual receipts and payments during settlement.
Remember: Dissolution questions are mainly about correct transfer, settlement and final distribution.

8. Share Capital

Share capital represents funds raised by a company by issuing shares. Shares may be equity or preference shares.

Issue Process

  1. Application
  2. Allotment
  3. Calls
  • Oversubscription may lead to rejection, refund or pro-rata allotment.
  • Calls in arrears are amounts not received when due.
  • Forfeiture occurs when shares are cancelled for non-payment under the applicable rules.
  • Forfeited shares may be reissued; the discount on reissue is subject to the prescribed limit.

9. Debentures

Debentures are long-term debt instruments issued by a company. Debenture holders are creditors, not owners.

  • Debentures can be issued at par, premium or discount according to the terms and applicable accounting rules.
  • Interest is payable according to the debenture terms and is a finance cost.
  • Redemption means repayment of debenture liability.

10. Financial Statements

Statement of Profit and Loss

Shows revenue, expenses and resulting profit/loss for a period.

Balance Sheet

Shows the financial position at a particular date through equity, liabilities and assets.

Notes to Accounts

Give detailed supporting information and accounting disclosures behind the financial statement figures.

11. Analysis of Financial Statements

  • Comparative statements: compare figures of two or more periods.
  • Common-size statements: convert items into percentages of a common base.
  • Trend analysis: studies movement over several periods using a base year.
  • Interpret results with the firm's size, industry and past trend in mind.

12. Accounting Ratios

Liquidity

Current Ratio = Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities
Quick Ratio = Quick Assets ÷ Current Liabilities

Solvency

Debt-Equity Ratio = Long-term Debt ÷ Shareholders' Funds

Profitability

Gross Profit Ratio = Gross Profit ÷ Revenue from Operations × 100
Net Profit Ratio = Net Profit ÷ Revenue from Operations × 100

Ratios should be compared with previous years, targets or industry information before drawing conclusions.

13. Cash Flow Statement

A Cash Flow Statement explains changes in cash and cash equivalents during a period.

Three Activities

  • Operating: cash from main revenue-generating activities.
  • Investing: purchase/sale of long-term assets and investments.
  • Financing: changes in share capital and borrowings, including relevant issue/redemption flows.

Indirect Method

Start with accounting profit, adjust non-cash and non-operating items, then adjust working-capital changes to determine operating cash flow.
