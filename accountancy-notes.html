1. Partnership Fundamentals

Partnership is a relationship between persons who agree to share profits of a business carried on by all or any one acting for all. A partnership deed records the agreed terms.

Important points

  • Profit-sharing ratio should be clearly known.
  • In the absence of an agreement, profits and losses are shared equally and interest rules follow the applicable partnership provisions.
  • Capital accounts may be fixed or fluctuating.
  • Profit and Loss Appropriation Account deals with distribution of profit among partners.
  • Past adjustments correct omitted or wrongly recorded items such as interest, salary or profit share.

2. Goodwill

Goodwill represents the value of a firm's reputation and its ability to earn higher profits than a comparable business.

Factors

  • Location, quality of products, customer loyalty, management, competition and expected future profits.

Valuation

Average Profit Goodwill = Average Profit × Number of Years' Purchase
Super Profit = Average Profit − Normal Profit
Super Profit Goodwill = Super Profit × Years' Purchase
Normal Profit = Capital Employed × Normal Rate / 100
Capitalisation Method: Capitalised Value of Average Profit − Actual Capital Employed

3. Change in Profit-Sharing Ratio

When existing partners change their ratio, one partner may sacrifice while another gains.

  • Sacrificing Ratio = Old Share − New Share.
  • Gaining Ratio = New Share − Old Share.
  • Goodwill adjustment is made between gaining and sacrificing partners according to the agreed treatment.
  • Revaluation records changes in asset values and liabilities.
  • Reserves and accumulated profits/losses are distributed according to the old ratio unless the question specifies otherwise.

4. Admission of a Partner

  • A new partner normally brings capital and may bring goodwill.
  • Determine the new profit-sharing ratio and sacrificing ratio.
  • Revalue assets and liabilities and distribute the resulting profit/loss among old partners in the old ratio.
  • Adjust reserves and accumulated profits/losses.
  • Prepare partners' capital/current accounts and the revised balance sheet.

5. Retirement or Death of a Partner

  • Determine the new ratio and gaining ratio of continuing partners.
  • Adjust goodwill, revaluation, reserves and accumulated profits/losses.
  • The retiring partner's amount due is settled through cash/bank or transferred to a loan account as required.
  • For death, calculate the deceased partner's share of profit up to the relevant date and other amounts due according to the question.

6. Dissolution of Partnership Firm

Dissolution closes the firm's books and settles assets, liabilities and partners' accounts.

  • Realisation Account records sale/transfer of assets and settlement of liabilities.
  • Realisation profit/loss is transferred to partners' capital accounts in their profit-sharing ratio.
  • External liabilities are settled before final distribution to partners, subject to the applicable rules.
  • Cash/Bank Account records receipts and payments during settlement.

7. Accounting for Share Capital

  • Share capital may include equity and preference shares.
  • Know the stages of issue: application, allotment and calls.
  • Oversubscription may require rejection, refund or pro-rata allotment according to the question.
  • Forfeiture occurs when a shareholder fails to pay amounts due and the company follows the prescribed accounting treatment.
  • Reissue of forfeited shares requires calculation of the permitted discount and transfer of remaining forfeiture balance as applicable.

8. Issue and Redemption of Debentures

  • Debentures are a form of long-term borrowing of a company.
  • They may be issued at par, premium or discount, subject to the applicable rules.
  • Interest on debentures is a finance cost and is treated according to the terms of issue.
  • Redemption means repayment of debenture liability according to the agreed terms.

9. Financial Statements of a Company

  • Statement of Profit and Loss reports income, expenses and profit/loss for the period.
  • Balance Sheet reports assets, equity and liabilities at the reporting date.
  • Notes to accounts provide supporting details and accounting disclosures.
  • Learn the prescribed presentation format and major headings used in company financial statements.

10. Analysis of Financial Statements

  • Comparative statements compare figures across periods and show absolute and percentage changes.
  • Common-size statements express items as percentages of a common base.
  • Trend analysis studies movement of figures over time.
  • Interpretation should consider the ratio, its trend and the business context rather than one number alone.

11. Accounting Ratios

Current Ratio = Current Assets / Current Liabilities
Quick Ratio = Quick Assets / Current Liabilities
Debt-Equity Ratio = Long-term Debt / Shareholders' Funds
Gross Profit Ratio = Gross Profit / Revenue from Operations × 100
Net Profit Ratio = Net Profit / Revenue from Operations × 100

Ratios help analyse liquidity, solvency and profitability. Always use the correct numerator and denominator from the question.

12. Cash Flow Statement

Cash flows are classified into three activities:

  • Operating: cash flows from the main revenue-generating activities.
  • Investing: purchase/sale of long-term assets and investments.
  • Financing: changes in capital and borrowings, such as issue/redemption and related financing flows.

Under the indirect method, start with accounting profit and adjust for non-cash items, non-operating items and changes in working capital to arrive at operating cash flow.

⭐ Exam Checklist

  • Learn formats before practising numerical questions.
  • Write working notes clearly.
  • Check ratios, signs, dates and profit-sharing ratios.
  • Practise partnership adjustments, share capital, ratios and cash flow regularly.
