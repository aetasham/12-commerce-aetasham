1. Nature and Significance of Management

Management is the process of planning, organising, staffing, directing and controlling resources to achieve objectives effectively and efficiently. Its objectives are organisational, social and personal.

Features

  • Goal oriented
  • Continuous process
  • Pervasive at all levels
  • Multidimensional
  • Group activity
  • Dynamic

Management is a science because it has organised knowledge, an art because it requires skill and practice, and a profession-like discipline because it uses specialised knowledge, though it is not a fully regulated profession.

Q1. Why is management important?
A. It helps achieve goals, improves efficiency, creates a dynamic organisation, develops employees and contributes to society.
Q2. What is coordination?
A. Coordination integrates individual and departmental efforts toward common objectives. It is continuous and pervasive.
Q3. Name the levels of management.
A. Top, middle and supervisory/operational management.

2. Principles of Management

Principles are broad and flexible guidelines for managerial decisions. Fayol's principles include division of work, authority and responsibility, discipline, unity of command, unity of direction, equity, initiative and esprit de corps.

Taylor emphasised science not rule of thumb, harmony not discord, cooperation not individualism and development of each person. Techniques include functional foremanship, standardisation, method study, motion study, time study, fatigue study and differential piece-rate system.

Q1. Unity of command means?
A. An employee should receive orders from one superior only.
Q2. What is scalar chain?
A. The formal line of authority from the highest to the lowest level.
Q3. Science not rule of thumb?
A. Taylor advocated replacing trial-and-error methods with systematic study and scientific methods.
Case practice: If a manager gives one employee conflicting orders through two supervisors, identify the violated principle and explain it.

3. Business Environment

Business environment is the totality of external forces influencing business decisions and performance. It is dynamic, uncertain, relative, interrelated and consists of specific and general forces.

Dimensions

Economic, social, technological, political and legal environment.

Q1. Why study business environment?
A. It helps identify opportunities and threats, gives first-mover advantage, assists planning, improves performance and helps firms adapt.
Q2. What is technological environment?
A. It includes changes in technology, methods, innovation and information systems affecting business.

4. Planning

Planning means deciding in advance what is to be done, how, when and by whom. It is goal-oriented, primary, pervasive, continuous, futuristic and involves decision-making.

Importance

  • Provides direction
  • Reduces uncertainty
  • Reduces overlapping and waste
  • Promotes innovative ideas
  • Facilitates decision-making
  • Provides standards for control

Types of Plans

Objectives, strategy, policy, procedure, method, rule, programme and budget.

Q1. What is strategy?
A. A comprehensive plan designed to achieve organisational objectives in view of environmental conditions.
Q2. State two limitations of planning.
A. It may create rigidity and cannot guarantee success; it may also be time-consuming and costly.

5. Organising

Organising identifies work, groups activities, assigns duties and establishes authority relationships.

Process

Identify/divide work → departmentalise → assign duties → establish reporting relationships.

Delegation involves authority, responsibility and accountability. Decentralisation is systematic dispersal of decision-making authority.

Q1. Delegation vs decentralisation?
A. Delegation is transfer of authority from superior to subordinate; decentralisation is wider systematic distribution of decision-making authority across levels.
Q2. Formal organisation?
A. The deliberately created structure of jobs, authority and relationships.

6. Staffing

Staffing is concerned with obtaining, developing, compensating and retaining suitable people.

Process: manpower planning, recruitment, selection, placement/orientation, training and development, appraisal, promotion/career planning and compensation.

Q1. Recruitment vs selection?
A. Recruitment attracts candidates; selection chooses the most suitable candidate.
Q2. Training vs development?
A. Training improves present-job skills; development prepares employees for broader future responsibilities.

7. Directing

Directing initiates action and guides employees. Elements are supervision, motivation, leadership and communication.

Maslow's needs: physiological, safety, social, esteem and self-actualisation. Leadership styles include autocratic, democratic and laissez-faire.

Q1. What is motivation?
A. The process of stimulating people to act toward desired goals.
Q2. Name elements of directing.
A. Supervision, motivation, leadership and communication.
Q3. What is communication?
A. The process of exchanging ideas, facts and information so that the message is understood.

8. Controlling

Controlling ensures actual performance conforms to planned standards and provides corrective action.

  1. Set standards
  2. Measure actual performance
  3. Compare with standards
  4. Analyse deviations
  5. Take corrective action
Q1. Why are planning and controlling inseparable?
A. Planning provides standards and controlling checks results against those standards and gives feedback for future planning.

9. Financial Management

Financial management concerns procurement and effective utilisation of funds.

Major Decisions

  • Investment decision
  • Financing decision
  • Dividend decision

Capital structure is the mix of debt and equity. Working capital supports day-to-day operations.

Q1. What is investment decision?
A. It determines where available funds should be invested, considering expected return and risk.
Q2. What is financing decision?
A. It decides the sources and mix of funds used by the business.

10. Financial Markets

Financial markets facilitate transfer of funds between savers and users.

  • Money market: short-term funds.
  • Capital market: long-term funds.
  • Primary market: new securities.
  • Secondary market: existing securities.

SEBI regulates the securities market and seeks investor protection and orderly development.

Q1. Primary vs secondary market?
A. Primary market deals with new securities issued by companies/government; secondary market provides trading of existing securities.

11. Marketing Management

Marketing identifies needs and creates, communicates and delivers value. Marketing mix consists of Product, Price, Place and Promotion.

  • Product: quality, design, branding, packaging, labelling and support.
  • Price: amount charged, affected by cost, demand and competition.
  • Place: channels and physical distribution.
  • Promotion: advertising, personal selling, sales promotion and public relations.
Q1. What is branding?
A. Giving a product a distinct name, sign, symbol, design or combination to identify it.
Q2. Advertising vs personal selling?
A. Advertising is paid non-personal communication through media; personal selling is direct interaction with prospective buyers.

12. Consumer Protection

Consumer protection safeguards consumers against unfair practices and helps them obtain information, safety and redressal.

Consumer Rights

Safety, information, choice, being heard, redressal and consumer education.

Responsibilities

Be quality-conscious, read labels, insist on bills, use products properly and seek redressal where necessary.

Q1. Why is consumer protection needed?
A. Consumers may face exploitation, misleading information, defective goods or deficient services; protection provides rights and remedies.
Q2. Why should consumers insist on a bill?
A. It provides proof of purchase and can support a complaint or claim for redressal.
