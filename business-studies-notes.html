1. Nature and Significance of Management

Management: the process of planning, organising, staffing, directing and controlling resources to achieve objectives effectively and efficiently.

  • Objectives: organisational, social and personal.
  • Importance: achieving goals, efficient resource use, adapting to change, employee development and societal contribution.
  • Management is a science because it has organised knowledge; an art because it requires practice and skill; and a profession because it has specialised knowledge and ethical expectations, though it is not a fully regulated profession.
  • Levels: top, middle and supervisory.
  • Coordination integrates individual and departmental efforts and is continuous and pervasive.

2. Principles of Management

Principles are broad guidelines that help managers make decisions and handle situations.

Fayol

Major principles include division of work, authority and responsibility, discipline, unity of command, unity of direction, subordination of individual interest, remuneration, centralisation/decentralisation, scalar chain, order, equity, stability of personnel, initiative and esprit de corps.

Taylor

  • Science, not rule of thumb
  • Harmony, not discord
  • Cooperation, not individualism
  • Development of each person to greatest efficiency
  • Functional foremanship and standardisation
  • Method, motion, time, fatigue and differential piece-rate ideas

3. Business Environment

Business environment is the totality of external forces that influence business decisions and performance.

  • Features: totality of external forces, specific and general forces, interrelatedness, dynamic nature, uncertainty and relativity.
  • Dimensions: economic, social, technological, political and legal.
  • Importance: identifies opportunities and threats, gives first-mover advantage, helps planning, improves performance and helps firms adapt.
  • Government policy changes can affect taxation, competition, trade and business operations.

4. Planning

Planning means deciding in advance what is to be done, how, when and by whom.

  • Features: goal-oriented, primary function, pervasive, continuous, futuristic and involves decision-making.
  • Importance: provides direction, reduces uncertainty, reduces overlapping, promotes innovation, facilitates decision-making and provides standards for control.
  • Limitations: rigidity, reduced creativity, time/cost, no guarantee of success and difficulty in a changing environment.

Types of plans: objectives, strategy, policy, procedure, method, rule, programme and budget.

5. Organising

Organising identifies work, groups activities, assigns duties and establishes authority relationships.

  • Steps: identify/divide work, departmentalise, assign responsibility and establish reporting relationships.
  • Structures: functional and divisional.
  • Formal organisation is deliberately created; informal organisation arises from social relationships.
  • Delegation includes authority, responsibility and accountability.
  • Decentralisation means systematic dispersal of decision-making authority throughout levels.

6. Staffing

Staffing means obtaining, developing and retaining suitable people.

  • Process: manpower planning, recruitment, selection, placement/orientation, training/development, appraisal, promotion/career planning and compensation.
  • Recruitment attracts candidates; selection chooses the most suitable candidate.
  • Training improves present-job skills; development prepares employees for broader future responsibilities.
  • Sources of recruitment may be internal or external.

7. Directing

Directing initiates action and guides employees toward organisational goals.

  • Elements: supervision, motivation, leadership and communication.
  • Maslow's hierarchy: physiological, safety, social, esteem and self-actualisation needs.
  • Financial incentives include pay, bonus and profit sharing; non-financial incentives include recognition, participation, status and career growth.
  • Leadership styles: autocratic, democratic and laissez-faire.
  • Communication may be formal/informal and upward/downward/horizontal.

8. Controlling

Controlling checks whether actual performance matches planned standards and enables corrective action.

  1. Set performance standards.
  2. Measure actual performance.
  3. Compare performance with standards.
  4. Analyse deviations.
  5. Take corrective action.

Planning and controlling are closely linked: plans provide standards and control provides feedback for future planning.

9. Financial Management

Financial management concerns procurement and effective use of funds.

  • Financial decisions: investment, financing and dividend.
  • Investment decision considers expected return and risk; working capital is required for day-to-day operations.
  • Capital structure is the mix of debt and equity.
  • Factors affecting financing include cost, risk, cash-flow position, control and market conditions.
  • Dividend decision balances shareholder returns with retained earnings needs.

10. Financial Markets

Financial markets facilitate transfer of funds between savers and users.

  • Money market deals mainly in short-term funds; capital market deals in long-term funds.
  • Primary market issues new securities; secondary market enables trading of existing securities.
  • SEBI regulates the securities market and seeks to protect investors and promote orderly development.
  • Common instruments include shares, debentures and government securities.

11. Marketing Management

Marketing is the process of identifying needs and creating, communicating and delivering value.

Marketing Mix — 4 Ps

  • Product: quality, design, branding, packaging, labelling and support.
  • Price: amount charged; influenced by cost, demand, competition and objectives.
  • Place: channels, physical distribution and logistics.
  • Promotion: advertising, personal selling, sales promotion and public relations.

Branding creates identity; packaging protects and promotes the product; labelling communicates information.

12. Consumer Protection

Consumer protection safeguards consumers from unfair practices and helps them obtain information, safety and redressal.

  • Rights: safety, information, choice, being heard, redressal and consumer education.
  • Responsibilities: be quality-conscious, read labels, insist on bills, use products properly and seek redressal when necessary.
  • Consumers can approach the appropriate consumer dispute redressal mechanism according to applicable law.
  • Quality marks/certifications and awareness can help informed purchasing.
