1. Nature and Significance of Management

Management is the process of getting work done effectively and efficiently through people. Its main functions are planning, organising, staffing, directing and controlling.

  • Effectiveness means achieving objectives.
  • Efficiency means achieving objectives with minimum resources.
  • Management helps achieve goals, develops people and adapts organisations to change.

2. Principles of Management

Principles are broad guidelines for managerial decisions. Fayol proposed principles such as division of work, authority and responsibility, discipline, unity of command, unity of direction and scalar chain.

Taylor developed scientific management, emphasising scientific study of work, standardisation and cooperation between management and workers.

3. Business Environment

Business environment consists of external forces affecting an organisation.

  • Economic
  • Social
  • Technological
  • Political
  • Legal

Understanding the environment helps identify opportunities, threats and changes in customer needs.

4. Planning

Planning means deciding in advance what is to be done, how, when and by whom. It gives direction, reduces uncertainty, avoids overlapping and provides standards for control.

Steps include setting objectives, developing premises, identifying alternatives, evaluating alternatives, selecting a course and implementing it.

5. Organising

Organising identifies work, groups activities, assigns duties and establishes reporting relationships.

  • Division of work
  • Departmentalisation
  • Assignment of responsibility
  • Establishing authority relationships

Delegation involves authority, responsibility and accountability.

6. Staffing

Staffing means finding, selecting, developing and retaining suitable people.

  • Manpower planning
  • Recruitment and selection
  • Training and development
  • Performance appraisal
  • Compensation and career development

7. Directing

Directing guides and motivates employees to achieve goals. It includes supervision, motivation, leadership and communication.

Motivation may be financial or non-financial. Leadership is the ability to influence people towards organisational goals. Communication transfers information and understanding.

8. Controlling

Controlling ensures actual performance is consistent with planned standards.

  1. Set performance standards.
  2. Measure actual performance.
  3. Compare actual performance with standards.
  4. Analyse deviations.
  5. Take corrective action.

9. Financial Management

Financial management deals with procurement and effective use of funds.

  • Investment decision: where funds should be invested.
  • Financing decision: sources of finance.
  • Dividend decision: distribution and retention of profit.

Capital structure, working capital and financial planning are important areas.

10. Financial Markets

Financial markets facilitate movement of funds between savers and users. Money market mainly deals with short-term funds. Capital market deals with long-term funds and includes securities markets.

11. Marketing Management

Marketing identifies customer needs and creates, communicates and delivers value.

Marketing Mix — 4 Ps

  • Product
  • Price
  • Place
  • Promotion

Promotion includes advertising, personal selling, sales promotion and public relations.

12. Consumer Protection

Consumer protection safeguards buyers from unfair trade practices and unsafe or defective goods/services.

Important rights include safety, information, choice, being heard, redressal and consumer education.
