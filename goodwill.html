1. Meaning of Goodwill

Goodwill is the value attached to a firm's reputation, customer relationships and other advantages that help it earn profits above what may normally be expected from a comparable business.

Factors affecting goodwill

  • Location and business environment
  • Quality of products and services
  • Customer loyalty
  • Management efficiency
  • Profitability and future earning capacity
  • Competition and market conditions

2. Why Goodwill is Valued

Goodwill may need valuation when partners change their profit-sharing arrangement, a new partner is admitted, an existing partner retires or dies, or another reconstitution event requires adjustment.

Exam point: Goodwill belongs to the business as an intangible advantage, but partnership questions often require adjustment between partners based on sacrifice and gain.

3. Average Profit Method

Average Profit = Total Adjusted Profits ÷ Number of Years
Goodwill = Average Profit × Years' Purchase

Adjust past profits for abnormal or non-recurring items before finding the average whenever the question requires it.

Example: Adjusted profits are ₹40,000, ₹50,000 and ₹60,000. Average profit = ₹50,000. At 3 years' purchase, goodwill = ₹1,50,000.

4. Weighted Average Profit Method

When recent years are considered more representative, different weights may be assigned to profits.

Weighted Average Profit = Total of (Profit × Weight) ÷ Total Weights
Goodwill = Weighted Average Profit × Years' Purchase
Example: Profits ₹30,000, ₹40,000 and ₹50,000 with weights 1, 2 and 3. Weighted profit = (30,000 + 80,000 + 1,50,000) ÷ 6 = ₹43,333.33 approximately.

5. Super Profit Method

Normal Profit = Capital Employed × Normal Rate of Return ÷ 100
Super Profit = Average Profit − Normal Profit
Goodwill = Super Profit × Years' Purchase
Example: Capital employed ₹5,00,000, normal return 10%, average profit ₹70,000. Normal profit = ₹50,000; super profit = ₹20,000. At 3 years' purchase, goodwill = ₹60,000.

6. Capitalisation Method

Capitalisation of Average Profit

Capitalised Value = Average Profit × 100 ÷ Normal Rate
Goodwill = Capitalised Value − Actual Capital Employed

Capitalisation of Super Profit

Goodwill = Super Profit × 100 ÷ Normal Rate

Use the method specifically asked for in the question and keep the normal rate and capital employed units consistent.

7. Hidden Goodwill

Hidden goodwill may be inferred when a new partner brings a known amount of capital for a known share of profit, while the question does not directly state the firm's goodwill.

Implied Total Capital = New Partner's Capital ÷ New Partner's Fraction of Total Capital

The difference between implied total capital and actual total capital, where appropriate under the question's assumptions, may indicate hidden goodwill.

Example: A new partner brings ₹40,000 for a 1/5 share. Implied total capital = ₹2,00,000. Compare this with the actual capital of the reconstituted firm before determining any implied goodwill.

8. Goodwill on Admission

When a new partner is admitted, the new partner normally compensates the sacrificing partners for the share of goodwill acquired, subject to the agreed accounting treatment.

Sacrificing Ratio = Old Share − New Share

Goodwill brought in by the new partner is generally credited to the sacrificing partners in their sacrificing ratio when the standard treatment applies.

9. Goodwill on Retirement or Death

When a partner retires or dies, continuing partners gain the outgoing partner's share. The outgoing partner is therefore entitled to the appropriate share of goodwill.

Gaining Ratio = New Share − Old Share

The gaining partners compensate the outgoing partner according to the applicable goodwill adjustment method.

10. Change in Profit-Sharing Ratio

For a change among existing partners, first calculate sacrifice or gain for each partner. Goodwill adjustment should then follow the terms of the question.

Sacrifice = Old Share − New Share
Gain = New Share − Old Share

11. Goodwill Treatment: Quick Logic

  • Admission: identify who sacrifices and adjust in favour of sacrificing partners.
  • Retirement/Death: identify who gains and compensate the outgoing partner.
  • Ratio change: compare old and new shares.
  • Valuation: use the method asked—average profit, weighted average, super profit or capitalisation.

12. Numerical Practice

Q1. Average profit ₹80,000 and years' purchase 3. Find goodwill.
Answer: ₹2,40,000.
Q2. Capital employed ₹6,00,000, normal return 10%, average profit ₹90,000. Find super profit.
Answer: Normal profit ₹60,000; super profit ₹30,000.
Q3. Super profit ₹25,000, normal return 10%. Find goodwill by capitalisation of super profit.
Answer: ₹2,50,000.

13. MCQ Practice

1. Goodwill is primarily associated with: A) Reputation and earning advantage B) Cash balance C) Trade payables D) Stock only
Answer: A
2. Under average profit method, goodwill equals: A) Average Profit × Years' Purchase B) Capital × Rate C) Sales ÷ Capital D) Super Profit ÷ Years
Answer: A
3. Super profit is: A) Average Profit − Normal Profit B) Capital − Profit C) Sales − Expenses D) Normal Profit − Average Profit
Answer: A
4. On admission, goodwill brought by a new partner generally benefits: A) Sacrificing partners B) Only new partner C) Creditors D) Customers
Answer: A
5. On retirement, goodwill adjustment is linked with: A) Gaining ratio B) Sales ratio C) Current ratio D) Debt ratio
Answer: A

15. Quick Revision

  • Average Profit → Average × Years' Purchase.
  • Normal Profit → Capital Employed × Normal Rate ÷ 100.
  • Super Profit → Average Profit − Normal Profit.
  • Capitalisation → Average Profit × 100 ÷ Normal Rate.
  • Admission → focus on sacrifice.
  • Retirement/Death → focus on gain.
  • Always read the question carefully for the exact goodwill treatment.
